# GaiaWorld Atlas — First Customer Pilot Kit

**Status:** Sales hypothesis. No performance benefit, customer acceptance, or revenue is established.

## The offer
**Geographic Evidence Briefing Pilot — proposed fixed fee $500.** Up to five non-sensitive study locations chosen with the customer, one agreed public scientific collection, geographically bounded observation counts, links to original records, timestamps, data-quality limitations, and a 30-minute findings review.

Not included: regulatory Phase I/II environmental site assessments, physical safety advice, validated hazard/risk prediction, continuous hosted monitoring, service-level guarantees, sensitive facilities, or legal/compliance certifications. Data availability and fit must be confirmed before promising deliverables.

**Acceptance criteria:** locations/radii/source agreed in writing; all underlying records retained in export; each cited observation traceable to its official source; disagreements and missing data documented; customer may reject the pilot if source availability makes the planned analysis unusable. Payment terms and any commercial agreement require founder approval.

## Five-minute live demonstration
1. **0:00–0:45 — Question.** “How long does it take to assemble a defensible source-linked briefing for several sites?” Ask the prospect how they do it now. Do not claim Atlas already saves time.
2. **0:45–1:30 — Live source.** Visit https://oluwafemidiakhoa.github.io/GaiaWorld-Atlas/ and show the selected official feed, its last successful retrieval, and source link. Explain that feeds are partial/provisional and browser refresh only works while open.
3. **1:30–2:45 — One site.** Enter a fictional or nonsensitive latitude/longitude and a *reasonable* radius. Generate a location briefing. Point out provenance, observation time, and the difference between proximity and exposure.
4. **2:45–3:45 — Multiple sites.** Add two or three fictional sites; compare counts and produce an export. Counts overlap and must not be interpreted as independent risk scores.
5. **3:45–4:15 — Trust.** Run the quality check. It catches selected structural/data issues; it does **not** verify scientific conclusions or guarantee completeness.
6. **4:15–5:00 — Discovery ask.** “Would your team use this to prepare an initial research brief, or does it miss a critical data source? If useful, who owns the budget and would a narrowly scoped paid evaluation make sense?”

## Qualification questions
- Which recurring geographic research decision takes your team the most time?
- Which original datasets, jurisdictions, and temporal windows are essential?
- What must a defensible output contain to be useful?
- Would public-source proximity counts be relevant, or do you need regulated site investigation and more sophisticated modeling?
- What would you compare Atlas against, and how will we measure speed and accuracy?
- Who can approve a small pilot, and what procurement/security conditions apply?

## Discovery outcome and next action
- **Problem + user + budget owner + feasible source:** propose a paid pilot with site/source requirements, sample deliverable and acceptance criteria.
- **Useful problem but no budget or insufficient source:** seek an unpaid usability evaluation with clear boundaries, not a false sale.
- **Requires certified risk/compliance analysis:** decline that scope rather than representing Atlas as fit for purpose.
- **No fit:** record why and use the finding to refine positioning.
